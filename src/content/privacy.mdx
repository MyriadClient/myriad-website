# Privacy policy

_Last updated: 26 November 2026_

Myriad is a free, open-source project run by **the Myriad project** ([MyriadClient](https://github.com/MyriadClient)
on GitHub). This page covers the website (myriadclient.dev), its API (api.myriadclient.dev), its maven repository
(maven.myriadclient.dev), the Myriad Library and the Myriad client itself. Because the code is public, you can check
everything below against what it actually does.

## The short version

- No accounts, no ads, no analytics, no tracking cookies, and nothing sold or shared for marketing.
- The Myriad client sends us nothing about you. It downloads the Myriad Library's list of addons when you browse or
  check for updates, and uploads a config only when you share one.
- If you list an addon in the Library, we keep your GitHub account's and repositories' names and ids and what our
  builder found in your releases.
- Your IP address reaches our servers when you use them, as it does for any website. We use it to deliver pages and to
  stop abuse, and we don't build profiles from it.

## The website

The site is static pages. It has no sign-in, no analytics and no third-party scripts. Its fonts are served from our own
server, and the docs search runs entirely in your browser.

- **Cloudflare** sits in front of the site, the API and maven to deliver them quickly and to block attacks. It processes
  your IP address and the details of each request (the page, your browser's user agent) for that purpose, under
  [Cloudflare's privacy policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/).
- **Our server** keeps ordinary request logs (IP address, time, page, browser) to keep it running and to investigate
  abuse. They're kept only briefly and are never used to track anyone.
- **Your browser** stores your light/dark choice for the docs in its local storage. It never leaves your device. See
  the [cookie policy](/docs/cookies).

## Shared configs

When you share a config from Myriad, the client uploads what you chose to share:
- its title, modules and settings (minus any setting an addon marks private, which Myriad leaves out before uploading
  and the server drops if sent);
- your theme, which may name its author (your Minecraft name, if you made it);
- control panels and wheels;
- the names and versions of your addons, Myriad and Minecraft.

- **Who can see it:** anyone with its code or link. That's the point of sharing, so don't put anything in a share you
  wouldn't post publicly.
- **What else is kept:** when it was made, how many times it's been viewed, and a hash of the delete key your client
  was given. No account or IP address is stored with it.
- **IP addresses** are used in memory to limit how fast one address can upload, read or guess codes, and are then
  forgotten. They're never written to the database.
- **How long:** until you delete it, from Myriad (`.share delete <code>`, with the key your client kept). If you've lost the
  key, ask us (below) with the share's code.

## The Myriad client

Myriad has no telemetry. It doesn't report what you do, which servers you play on or anything about your computer.
It contacts other services only for these reasons:

- **Update checks.** When the game starts (you can turn this off in Addons), Myriad asks GitHub's API about new
  releases of Myriad and your addons. An addon with its own update feed is checked at its feed's address instead. Those
  services see your IP address and which project is asked about, under their own policies
  ([GitHub's](https://docs.github.com/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement)). A private feed's
  access token, if you give one, is sent only to that feed.
- **Sharing a config**, only when you do, as above.
- **Adding an addon from a link**, only to the address you give.
- **The Myriad Library.** When you press Browse in Addons, and with each update check, Myriad downloads the Library's
  list from our API as one file. Nothing is sent with it but the ordinary request (your IP address, as above), and
  what you search for stays in the game. Installing or updating an addon from the Library downloads its jar from the
  developer's GitHub release, under GitHub's policy.

The Myriad cape is marked with a spare bit in your skin settings, which other players' clients see in-game. No server
is involved. Addons are made by their own developers and may do things Myriad doesn't: check their own policies.

## The Myriad Library

The [Myriad Library](/library/) lists addons that Myriad has checked. This section applies from when it opens.

**Players** need nothing to use it and give us nothing: the game downloads the list (above), and the website's
`/library` pages are static like the rest of the site.

**Developers** list an addon by installing the Myriad Library GitHub App on its repositories. GitHub then tells us
about those repositories and their releases, and we keep:

- the GitHub account the App is installed on (its id and name) and each repository's id, name and whether it's
  private;
- the releases you publish (tag, jar address, when), each build's result and our builder's report on it (hashes, what
  the inspection found, why it was or wasn't listed), and the maintainer's decisions;
- what you show players: your addon's name, description, author, icon and source repository;
- the ids of GitHub's notifications, to ignore repeats.

We don't receive your source code, except that closed-source addons are built by a private builder with a token that
reads one repository once. That copy of your source exists only inside that build, and the archive of it is kept
privately for a day for the review. The open-source builder's run logs are public on GitHub, like your source.

Signed listings, releases and withdrawals are published in the Library's list, and stay checkable by every game for
as long as the Library runs: they name your addon, the GitHub ids of its repository and owner, and its keys. The rest is kept while your
addon is listed. Once it's gone, you can ask us to delete it (below), except what's needed to show why a listing was
refused or withdrawn.

We use this only to run the Library: to build and check releases, show your addon, and settle disputes about it. It's
never sold or used for marketing.

## Children

Nothing here asks for personal information, and we don't knowingly collect any from children. We never ask for or
store your age or date of birth. Signing in (above) is for people aged 16 or over, and an account we learn belongs to
someone younger will be deleted. The client and the site work for everyone without one.

## Signing in, for invite-only addons

Signing in is optional, and only needed to install an addon its developer invited you to. You sign in from the game:
it proves which Minecraft account you are through Mojang's own check (the one joining a server uses), so your password
and your Minecraft login go only to Mojang, never to us. We keep:

- your Minecraft account's UUID and name;
- each session: a random token (we keep only its hash), a label like "Myriad on Linux", when it was made and last used.
  A session ends after 30 days unused, when you sign out, or when you end it from another session;
- the invite-only addons you've been invited to (from which invite, and when), and each time you download one (kept
  30 days, to stop codes being shared around);
- for each invite, only its hash, how many uses it has left and when it expires: never the code itself.

The developer of an addon you're invited to sees your Minecraft name and UUID, and when you were invited, so they can
manage who has access. There are no cookies: the game sends its session token itself. Signing out (`.account logout`,
or Sign out in Browse) ends the session here and on our side; ask us (below) to delete the rest.


## Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask what we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, or object to how it's used.
Under the GDPR and the UK GDPR, our legal basis for the server logs and rate limits is our legitimate interest in
running the services securely, for shares it's that you asked us to publish them, and for the Library it's that you
asked us to list your addon (and our legitimate interest in keeping the Library safe). As we hold almost nothing that
identifies anyone, most requests will be about a share. You can also complain to your local data-protection authority.

**Contact:** open an issue at
[github.com/MyriadClient/myriad-api/issues](https://github.com/MyriadClient/myriad-api/issues). Issues are public, so
include only what's needed (a share's code is enough to find it), never personal details.

## Changes

If this changes, the new version goes here with a new date first. Anything that collects more (like accounts) will be
described here before it starts.
