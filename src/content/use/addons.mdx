# Addons and updates

Modules come from **addons**: ordinary Fabric mods made for Myriad. Install one by putting its jar in `mods` next to
Myriad's.

| Addon | |
|---|---|
| [Myriad Essentials](https://github.com/MyriadClient/myriad-essentials) | the stock modules and HUD elements |
| [Myriad Omarchy](https://github.com/MyriadClient/myriad-omarchy) | themes that follow your Omarchy system theme |

## The Addons window

Open **Addons** from the launcher. It's one list: **Installed** first, then what else the **Myriad Library** offers. The
search at the top finds addons in both by name, author or module, and **Add from link** sits beside it. Each card shows
an addon's icon and what it is. Open it for **What's new**, in the same place on every card, then **Details**: who made
it, its id, how long it took to start (to find the one slowing your game's start) and the key its updates are signed
with. Its own settings follow, and its actions sit along the card's foot: its source, update, auto update, remove. What
an addon costs while the game runs is in the Profiler.

**Updates**: Myriad checks each addon's releases. When there's a new version, a badge shows it, the card shows what's
new when the Library knows, and **Update** installs it (it takes effect the next time you start the game). The switch in
the title bar turns checking off; the button beside it checks now, and **Update all** appears when there's more than one.
From chat: `.update check`, `.update all`.

**Auto update**: off for every addon until you turn it on, on its card. It's only offered for addons whose updates are
checked against a signature: those from the Library, Myriad itself, and addons that sign their releases. An update it
finds is downloaded and checked the same way, then installs the next time you start the game; it never restarts
anything. With any addon on auto update, a long session checks again every six hours.

**Remove**: on an addon's card, after you confirm. Its jar leaves the mods folder when you quit (a copy is kept in
`myriad/updates/old`), so the addon stays until then; **Keep it** takes that back. Myriad itself can't be removed here.

## The Myriad Library

The **Myriad Library** section of the Addons window lists the [Myriad Library](/library/)'s addons you don't have yet:
addons by other developers that Myriad has checked. Each card shows what's new in its latest release and **What it
adds**, every module with what it does, so you know what you'd install. Searching for a module narrows that list to the
match. Pick one and press **Install**: Myriad downloads it and checks every signature first, and nothing is written
unless they all hold. It loads the next time you start the game.

- **Verified** addons are open source. Myriad's builder built the release from its public source and checked the jar
  is exactly that build.
- **Reviewed** addons are closed source. Myriad's maintainer reviewed the release.

Every release is signed by its developer and by the Library, and Myriad checks both itself before installing, with
keys built into Myriad: neither this website nor Myriad's server could hand you a different jar. The badges mean
Myriad checked the addon, not that it's perfect: an addon can still have bugs, so install what you trust.

An addon installed from the Library takes its updates **only through the Library**, so a release that wasn't checked
never reaches you, even from the addon's own GitHub. Its card says so. **Unpin** on the card goes back to updating from
the addon's own source. An addon you already had gets its badge when your jar is exactly a listed release, but keeps
updating from its own source: only installing from the Library pins an addon to the Library.

Myriad reads the Library as one file when you open the Addons window or check for updates. What you search for never
leaves the game. If the Library can't be reached, the window shows the copy it last read. Once that copy is two days old, nothing is
installed or updated from it until Myriad reads a fresh one.

### Invite-only addons

An addon marked **Invite only** can be installed by the players its developer invited. **Sign in with Minecraft** once,
at the foot of the Addons window or on the addon's card: the game proves which Minecraft account you are the way joining a server does, so your password and
login go only to Mojang. Then enter the code its developer gave you (`myr-…`) and press **Redeem and install**: the code
is checked and the addon installed in one step. It updates through the Library like any other (if you're signed out
when an update comes, its card offers **Sign in and update**), and you see its changelog even when its developer keeps
that from everyone else. You stay signed in for 30 days of use, and at most 90
days, then sign in again. `.account` shows who you're signed in as, and `.account logout` (or Sign out at the foot of
the Addons window) signs you out. What's kept is in the [privacy policy](/docs/privacy/).

## Updates and signing

**Signed updates.** An addon's **Details** show the key its updates are signed with, and its **Source** button where
they come from. A signed addon takes
only updates signed with its developer's key, so even someone who takes over its GitHub account or website can't push
you a different jar: a release that isn't signed with that key is refused. Myriad's own updates are signed by its
maintainer, offline, after checking the released jar is exactly what its source builds. An unsigned addon updates as
before, and is only as safe as wherever it's released.

**Adding an addon from a link**: **Add from link** at the top of the Addons window (or `.update add <link>` in chat) takes an addon's
GitHub repository or its update feed's address. Myriad downloads and checks it first and shows what it is, where it's
from, and the fingerprint of the key it's signed with; it's installed only when you say so, and loads the next time
you start the game. If its developer publishes their key's fingerprint, check it matches: every later update will need
that key. An unsigned addon is marked in red: whoever controls where it's hosted decides what you get.

**Private addons**: for an addon from a private repository or feed, paste the access token its developer gave you into
the token field when adding it. If you need to add or replace one later, **Access token** appears on its card once a
token is saved or its source can't be read. It's kept in `myriad/updates/sources.json`, sent only to that source, and
never shown or copied back out. (Invite-only Library addons never need one: they update with your sign-in.)

If an addon fails to start, Myriad takes out everything it added and carries on without it. The Addons window shows
why it failed.
